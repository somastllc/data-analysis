*On screen: chapter card, then the five lesson titles listing down as they are named.*

Five lessons, and not one of them asks you to analyze anything. That is deliberate.

Chapter one is vocabulary: the shortest possible answer to the question, what am I actually working with?

You will learn what data is: raw facts, before anybody has made sense of them. And how that differs from information, and from insight. In ordinary conversation those three words get used interchangeably. In this field they mean different things, and the distance between them is the whole job.

Then the types data comes in. Where it comes from, and what that tells you about whether to trust it. What "bad data" actually means in specific, nameable ways, because "the data is bad" is not a useful sentence, and "this customer is listed as 200 years old" is.

And last, why any of this is worth your time.

This is the vocabulary everything else in data work assumes you already have.
