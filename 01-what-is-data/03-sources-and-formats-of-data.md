# Sources and Formats of Data

**Chapter:** 1 — What Is Data?  
**Lesson:** 3 of 5  
**By the end:** You can identify where a dataset came from, what format it is in, and whether it is primary or secondary.

## Overview

You receive a file from a colleague and need to decide whether you can trust it and how to open it. That decision depends on where the data came from and how it was stored.

### Common sources of data

Data can be collected in many ways:

- **Manual entry** - someone types data into a form or spreadsheet
- **Business systems** - sales, inventory, or HR software
- **Websites and apps** - clicks, sign-ups, purchases
- **Sensors and devices** - weather stations, fitness trackers, machines
- **Surveys and interviews** - customer or employee feedback
- **Public datasets** - government or research data shared openly

The source affects data quality. Data from a reliable business system may be more consistent than data typed manually into a spreadsheet.

### Primary vs secondary data

**Primary data** is collected firsthand for a specific purpose: a shop owner recording daily sales in a notebook, or a school conducting its own student survey.

**Secondary data** was collected by someone else and is reused for a new purpose: government census reports, industry research, or sales data exported from another department.

Primary data is often more relevant to your exact question. Secondary data can save time, but you must check whether it fits your needs and whether you trust the original source.

### Common data formats

A **format** is how data is stored or shared.

| Format | Common use |
|---|---|
| **CSV** | Simple tabular data in text files |
| **Excel (.xlsx)** | Spreadsheets with formulas and formatting |
| **JSON** | Web and application data exchange |
| **SQL databases** | Structured business data stored in tables |
| **PDF / Word** | Reports and documents, often harder to analyze directly |

As a beginner, you will most often encounter spreadsheets, CSV files, and database tables.

### Why source and format matter

Ask two questions about any dataset before you use it. **Where did this come from?** tells you whether to trust it. **What format is it in?** tells you what it will cost you to open.

The two answers are independent, which is what makes both questions worth asking:

- Sales data from a company database usually answers both well - trusted source, easy to open
- A PDF report can be entirely trustworthy and still cost you a day to get the numbers out of
- Survey responses can arrive in a clean spreadsheet and still be full of missing answers

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Data source** | Where data originally comes from |
| **Primary data** | Data collected firsthand for a specific purpose |
| **Secondary data** | Data collected by others and reused for a new purpose |
| **Data format** | The way data is stored or shared |
| **CSV** | A plain-text format for tabular data |
