Most people picture data work as the analysis. The analysis is one step out of seven, and it is rarely the longest.

Chapter three is the process in order, from somebody asking a vague question to somebody making a decision. Six lessons, one step at a time.

It starts before any data is touched, turning a request like "the numbers look off, can you check?" into a question that actually has an answer.

Then collecting data, and noticing when the easiest data to get is not the data that answers anything.

Then cleaning, which is where most of this field spends most of its time and almost none of its enthusiasm. Then analysis and visualization. Then communicating the result, which is where correct findings go to die if you get it wrong.

And finally the step people skip: naming what you assumed, and what your analysis cannot prove.

Every field in the last chapter runs these same steps. What changes is what comes out the end. A report, a dashboard, a model. What does not change is the order.

You know what data is, and who works with it. This is how the work actually runs.
