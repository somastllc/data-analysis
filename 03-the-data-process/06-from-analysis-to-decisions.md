# From Analysis to Decisions

**By the end:** You can state the assumptions and limitations behind an analysis, and connect a finding to a decision.

## Overview

Every analysis rests on things you did not check. Naming them is not a weakness. It is the difference between a finding people act on and one they quietly distrust. The final step is turning insights into action.

### From insight to action

Analysis should lead to a decision or next step.

Examples:

- Insight: "Customers who pay by digital wallet abandon the order form more often than those who pay on delivery."
  - Decision: Shorten the payment step

- Insight: "How long support takes to reply predicts whether a customer leaves."
  - Decision: Put more staff on the weekend shift

### Assumptions and limitations

Every analysis depends on assumptions.

An **assumption** is something believed to be true for the analysis to hold.

Examples:

- The data is accurate enough to trust
- The sample represents the full population
- Past patterns will continue in the future
- No important variables were left out

A **limitation** is a weakness or boundary in the data or method. Common limitations include:

- Missing data
- Small sample size
- Short time period
- Data collected from only one source

### Putting a model into use is also a decision

Not every result ends in a meeting. Sometimes the output is a system that goes on making the same decision thousands of times, long after everyone involved moved on.

That raises the stakes on the assumptions above rather than removing them: one nobody wrote down is now running unattended, on people who never saw the analysis.

### Why decisions still need judgment

Data supports decisions, but it does not replace human judgment.

Managers must consider:

- Business context
- Cost and resources
- Ethics and fairness
- Risks not captured in the data

### Example

An analysis shows that a discount increases sales but reduces profit.

Possible decisions:

- Continue the discount to gain market share
- Reduce the discount to protect profit
- Test a smaller discount and measure results

The analysis narrows three options to three trade-offs. Choosing between them is not a data question, and pretending it is will only disguise who actually made the decision.
