# From Analysis to Decisions

**Chapter:** 3 — The Data Analysis Process  
**Lesson:** 6 of 6  
**By the end:** You can state the assumptions and limitations behind an analysis, and connect a finding to a decision.

## Overview

Every analysis rests on things you did not check. Naming them is not a weakness — it is the difference between a finding people act on and one they quietly distrust. The final step in the analysis process is turning insights into action.

### From insight to action

Analysis should lead to a decision or next step.

Examples:

- Insight: "Mobile users abandon checkout more often."
  - Decision: Simplify the mobile checkout flow

- Insight: "Support response time predicts cancellation."
  - Decision: Hire more weekend support staff

Without action, analysis becomes interesting but useless.

### Assumptions and limitations

Every analysis depends on assumptions.

Examples:

- The data is accurate enough to trust
- The sample represents the full population
- Past patterns will continue in the future
- No important variables were left out

You should also report **limitations**, such as:

- Missing data
- Small sample size
- Short time period
- Data collected from only one source

Honest reporting makes your work more credible.

### Why decisions still need judgment

Data supports decisions, but it does not replace human judgment.

Managers must consider:

- Business context
- Cost and resources
- Ethics and fairness
- Risks not captured in the data

The best decisions combine data with experience and responsibility.

### Example

An analysis shows that a discount increases sales but reduces profit.

Possible decisions:

- Continue the discount to gain market share
- Reduce the discount to protect profit
- Test a smaller discount and measure results

The data provides evidence. Leaders choose the strategy.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Assumption** | Something believed to be true for the analysis to hold |
| **Limitation** | A weakness or boundary in the data or method |
| **Decision-making** | Choosing an action based on available evidence |
