You check your phone and see a mobile money transfer of $25, a weather app showing 32°C, and a message from school about your attendance. Each of those is a piece of data, a small fact about something that happened. Before you analyze data, build dashboards, or choose a career in data, you need a clear answer to a simple question: what is data?

1 — Raw facts

Data: raw facts about the world

Data is a collection of facts, measurements, or observations about the world. On its own, data usually does not tell a full story. It is the raw material we use to understand what is happening.

04 / 12

1 — Raw facts

Examples of data

  • $25 — a mobile money transfer amount
  • $2.50 per kg — the market price of rice
  • present — a student’s attendance record
  • $49.99 — a product price
  • 32°C — a temperature reading

Each example is a single fact. Collect enough of them and patterns start to appear.

2 — Not the same thing

Data vs information vs insight

These three terms are related, but they are not the same.

TermMeaningExample
DataRaw factsSales were $1,200, $980 and $1,450 on three days
InformationData organized to answer a basic questionSales increased from Monday to Wednesday
InsightA useful conclusion that can guide actionPromotions on Wednesday may be driving higher sales

3 — Every day

Where you see data every day

Data is not only found in spreadsheets or databases. You create and use data constantly:

  • Mobile money transactions and bank transfers
  • School grades and attendance records
  • Market prices at a local shop
  • Likes and comments on social media
  • Steps tracked by a fitness app
  • Weather forecasts

Businesses, schools, hospitals, and governments all rely on data to make decisions.
