Chapter 1 · What Is Data? · Lesson 1 of 5

What is data?

You check your phone and see a mobile money transfer of $25, a weather app showing 32°C, and a message from school about your attendance. Each of those is a piece of data - a small fact about something that happened. Before you analyze data, build dashboards, or choose a career in data, you need a clear answer to a simple question: what is data?

By the end

You can explain what data is, and tell data, information and insight apart.

Data: raw facts about the world

Data is a collection of facts, measurements, or observations about the world. On its own, data usually does not tell a full story. It is the raw material we use to understand what is happening.

  • $25 - a mobile money transfer amount
  • $2.50 per kg - the market price of rice
  • present - a student's attendance record
  • $49.99 - a product price
  • 32°C - a temperature reading

Each example is a single fact. By itself, it may not mean much. But when we collect many facts together, patterns start to appear.

Data vs information vs insight

These three terms are related, but they are not the same. The difference between them is, more or less, the whole job.

TermMeaningExample
DataRaw facts Sales were $1,200, $980 and $1,450 on three days
InformationData organized to answer a basic question Sales increased from Monday to Wednesday
InsightA useful conclusion that can guide action Promotions on Wednesday may be driving higher sales

Plate I - from facts to a decision

Each step throws information away and adds meaning. You cannot skip one: an insight with no data underneath it is just an opinion.

Think of it like cooking.

Data

The ingredients, before anyone has decided what to make.

Information

A prepared meal you can actually understand.

Insight

Knowing what to cook next time, based on what people liked.

In data work, your goal is to move from raw data toward meaningful insight.

Where you see data every day

Data is not only found in spreadsheets or databases. You create and use data constantly:

  • Mobile money transactions and bank transfers
  • School grades and attendance records
  • Market prices at a local shop
  • Likes and comments on social media
  • Steps tracked by a fitness app
  • Weather forecasts

Businesses, schools, hospitals and governments all rely on data to make decisions. That is why data skills are valuable across many industries.

Key terms

DataRaw facts, measurements, or observations
InformationData that has been organized to convey meaning
InsightA meaningful conclusion, supported by data, that helps guide a decision
