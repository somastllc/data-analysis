*On screen: nothing for the first two lines. Course title card at "That is what this course is for."*

You have heard that data is valuable. Maybe that it is the new oil, or that every company is a data company now. None of that tells you what data actually is, what people do with it all day, or whether you could do it too.

That is what this course is for.

This is Data Fundamentals for Beginners. It assumes you know nothing about data. It also assumes you have no tools. You will not install anything, open anything, or write a line of code. Not once.

That is deliberate. Before Python, before SQL, before spreadsheets and dashboards, you need the vocabulary. You need to hear someone say "the pipeline broke", or "that chart is misleading", or "we need a data engineer for this", and know exactly what they mean.

*On screen: six-chapter map, building as each is named.*

Six chapters, twenty-seven lessons.

Chapter one answers what data actually is. Chapter two maps the field: analytics, business intelligence, data science, machine learning, engineering, and how they fit together. Chapter three walks the process, from a vague request to a decision somebody acts on. Chapter four covers the tools and the jobs. Chapter five is ethics. Chapter six puts all of it through one business problem, start to finish.

One note about chapter five. It covers ethics: privacy, consent, bias, and the rules that catch problems before they ship. It is the part most data training skips, and it is easier to build the habit now, before you have the tools to do damage with. Do not skip it.

By the end of this course you will not be able to analyze a dataset. You will be able to tell whether one is worth analyzing, ask the question that makes it useful, and recognize when somebody is using data to mislead you.

That is the foundation. Everything else in data work is built on top of it.
