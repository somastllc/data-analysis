*On screen: the five terms appearing one per beat, then the seven lesson titles.*

Analytics. Data analysis. Data science. Machine learning. Data engineering.

Those five terms get used as though they were interchangeable. They are not, and the people who use them that way are usually the ones asking you for something impossible.

Chapter two is the map. Seven lessons.

You will learn the four types of analytics - what happened, why it happened, what might happen next, and what to do about it - and why a question about the past takes completely different work from a question about the future.

Then the fields and systems in turn. What data analysis does. What makes a problem data science, even when it never produces a model. When machine learning is actually necessary, and why it cannot work without past examples. Where data lives and how it moves through a pipeline. And what data engineers build that everybody else quietly depends on.

The last lesson is the one that matters most. It takes a single event - one customer cancelling a subscription - and shows six different contributors touching it, not one of whom could have handled it alone.

You know what data is. This chapter is who handles it.
