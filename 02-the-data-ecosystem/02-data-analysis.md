# Data Analysis

**By the end:** You can explain how data analysis uses data to answer questions and help people decide what to do.

## Overview

A shop owner notices that one product sells out every week while another sits on the shelf. She wants to understand why and decide what to order next. This is where **data analysis** becomes useful.

### What is data analysis?

**Data analysis** means looking at data, cleaning it, organizing it, and using it to find useful answers.

Data analysis may involve:

- Looking at how sales change
- Comparing results in different places
- Finding out why customers leave
- Preparing reports for managers

Data analysis often answers questions like:

- Which products sold the most?
- Why did the money earned go down in March?
- Which customer group makes the most profit?

### What data analysis can produce

The result depends on the question. Data analysis can produce:

- A finding that explains what happened
- A report that shows what the data found
- A metric that tracks something important
- A dashboard that keeps results visible over time

A **KPI (key performance indicator)** is a metric used to measure progress toward a goal.

Regular dashboards and reports are often called **business intelligence (BI)**. Companies may use different names for this work, but the goal is the same: help people see useful information and make decisions.

### Example

A wholesaler supplies shops in Mogadishu, Hargeisa, and Kismayo, and wants to know how each is doing.

- The analysis compares six months of orders and returns
- It finds that the Kismayo shops order the same amount as the others but send back much more unsold stock
- The result is shared in a report and a dashboard showing each city's weekly orders, returns, and top products

The analysis answered the manager's question and helped her decide what to order next.
